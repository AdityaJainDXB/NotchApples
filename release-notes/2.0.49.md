**New**
- 🌬 **Weather in the Plane menu.** A new **Weather** panel: a **crosswind slider from 0 to 90 kt** (from the left or the right), a **headwind / tailwind slider** (±30 kt), and **turbulence** from None through Light, Moderate and Severe to Extreme. Quick presets: **Calm**, **Breezy**, **Crosswind landing**, **Storm**, **Hurricane**, or **Random** like before. It applies to every mode and is remembered. The wind box on the panel now reads like a real one: "Wind 270° 25 kt, gusting 33", the cross and head components, and your ground speed. At 90 kt a Cessna lifts off after a couple of seconds and a parked plane weathervanes into the wind.
- 🛬 **Much more realistic touchdowns and landing gear.** Every wheel is a tyre on an oil-and-gas strut: it stiffens as it compresses, damps harder going down than coming back up, and can bottom out. Tyres push sideways with a real slip angle, so a crabbed crosswind landing squeals, straightens and weathercocks. The wheels spin up with a chirp and a puff of smoke at touchdown, a locked wheel skids and can burst (light planes; airliners and jets have anti-skid), and you get differential braking from the rudder, nosewheel steering, ground spoilers on airliners and **reverse thrust on X**. The 747, 777, 787, A350 and A380 have **tilting bogie trucks** that level out on touchdown. Bounces depend on sink rate and attitude: porpoising when nose-first, a roll when one wheel lands first, wingtip and nacelle scrapes when banked. A very hard landing damages a strut for good (shimmy and a pull; STRUT on the panel). A message rates every touchdown: "Nice touchdown 1.2 m/s, 98 m past the numbers", "Bounced 5.6 m/s".
- 💥 **Even more realistic crashes.** The plane now breaks into **a nose, a centre fuselage, a tail, wing panels, up to four engines and gear legs**, which tear off where they really would (wing roots, pylons, tips) and each tumble, bounce and slide on their own. A shallow, fast impact **skips** across the ground before it breaks up. **Fire** needs a torn fuel tank and a spark, so it doesn't always start, and the fireball follows how much fuel is left. A wreck leaves a gouge in the ground and flung dirt, smoke drifts with the wind, and a ditched plane floats, then sinks slowly. The camera drops into a short slow motion at the big impact, and the sound has layered crunches, grinding, crackling fire and a fading engine. The crash report adds the impact angle, peak g, the state of the cabin, what broke off, and how far the wreck slid.
